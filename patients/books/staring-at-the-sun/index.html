Mortality · meaning

Staring at the Sun

by Irvin D. Yalom, MD

Psychiatrist Irvin Yalom explores death anxiety with warmth and clinical wisdom. His central proposal is not that we eliminate fear, but that confronting finitude can clarify values, deepen relationships, and awaken us to life.

01

The big ideas

  • Death anxiety can appear indirectly as worry, avoidance, regret, or a sense of unlived life.
  • Connection and the influence we leave in others—what Yalom calls rippling—can counter isolation.
  • Awareness of limited time can help us choose more deliberately and postpone less.
02

What you can try

  • Name one regret you can still act on.
  • Have one conversation you have been postponing.
  • Ask what you would change if you were protecting your limited time more honestly.
03

Read critically

  • This is reflective reading, not a workbook or treatment protocol.
  • It may intensify distress during acute grief, panic, severe depression, or a new life-threatening diagnosis.
  • Professional support is appropriate when death anxiety becomes intrusive or disabling.
04

Questions to discuss

  • Is fear of death shaping a current decision or avoidance?
  • What relationships or contributions give my life continuity?
  • Would therapy help me approach this safely and constructively?
