Check your heart rhythm

For palpitations, a racing or skipping heartbeat, or keeping an eye on atrial fibrillation (AFib).

No prescription

KardiaMobile

A small pad you hold for 30 seconds. It records an ECG on your phone and tells you if it looks like AFib, a slow rhythm or a fast one.

What to knowRecord it while you feel the symptom, then send it to us. A normal reading with ongoing symptoms still needs a visit.

About $80–$150Learn more ↗
Some features FDA-cleared

Smartwatches with an ECG app

Apple Watch, Samsung Galaxy Watch, Fitbit, Garmin and Withings ScanWatch have FDA-cleared ECG apps and alerts for an irregular rhythm.

What to knowAn alert is a reason to call us, not a diagnosis. Sleep stages, “stress” and similar scores are estimates, not medical tests.

About $100–$800Learn more ↗
Some features FDA-cleared

Withings Body Scan

A bathroom scale with a handle. Its ECG is FDA-cleared to flag possible AFib while you weigh yourself.

What to knowUseful if you weigh yourself daily anyway. The body composition numbers are estimates.

About $400–$600Learn more ↗

Check your blood pressure

Home readings are the best way to know your real blood pressure.

No prescription

A validated upper-arm cuff

Any automatic upper-arm cuff listed on validatebp.org, in the right size for your arm.

What to knowTake two readings in the morning and two at night for a week before your visit and bring them in. This is the one I recommend most.

About $40–$100Learn more ↗
No prescription

Hilo band

A wristband that estimates blood pressure through the day while you sit still. You set it up with a regular cuff, which comes with it.

What to knowInteresting for patterns, including at night. Check any number that would change your treatment with a regular cuff.

About $250–$300Learn more ↗
Some features FDA-cleared

Apple Watch hypertension alerts

The watch looks at 30 days of data and may tell you that you might have high blood pressure.

What to knowIf you get an alert, check with a cuff for a week. No alert does not mean your blood pressure is normal.

Built into newer watchesLearn more ↗

Sleep and snoring

Loud snoring, gasping or very tired days can mean sleep apnea, which is treatable.

Some features FDA-cleared

Sleep apnea alerts on a watch or ring

Apple Watch, Samsung Galaxy Watch and the Samsung Galaxy Ring can flag signs of moderate to severe sleep apnea.

What to knowAn alert means ask us about a sleep test. No alert does not rule sleep apnea out.

Built into some devicesLearn more ↗
Needs a prescription

Home sleep tests

Wear sensors at home for one or more nights instead of sleeping in a lab. Examples include Wesper, Withings Sleep Rx and Sunrise.

What to knowWe order these. Tell us if you snore loudly, stop breathing at night or are always tired.

Usually covered by insuranceLearn more ↗
Needs a prescription

Sleep apps that treat insomnia

SleepioRx and Somryst teach CBT-I, the best treatment for long-term insomnia, through an app.

What to knowWorks better than sleeping pills over time. Ask us for a prescription.

Prescription; coverage variesLearn more ↗
Not FDA-reviewed

Oura and Whoop trackers

A ring or band that tracks sleep, resting heart rate, heart rate variability and temperature, and gives you daily scores. (Whoop’s ECG is FDA-cleared; the rest is wellness.)

What to knowGood for spotting your own patterns, like how alcohol or late meals affect your night. Sleep stages and “readiness” are estimates; the trend matters more than any one night.

$200–$500, plus a subscriptionLearn more ↗
Not FDA-reviewed

Eight Sleep

A mattress cover that cools or warms each side of the bed through the night and tracks your sleep.

What to knowGreat if you sleep hot, have night sweats, or you and your partner like different temperatures. Expensive. Not a treatment for sleep apnea.

Elemind headband

A headband that reads your brain waves and plays sounds timed to them to help you fall asleep.

What to knowSmall company studies show people fall asleep a bit faster. Try CBT-I first; this could be an add-on.

Check your blood sugar

Sensors you wear on your arm for 2 weeks that show your glucose all day.

No prescription

Over-the-counter glucose sensors

Dexcom Stelo and Abbott Lingo or Libre Rio, for adults who don’t use insulin. No prescription needed.

What to knowGreat for prediabetes or type 2 diabetes: try two weeks and see what meals, walks and sleep do. Rises after meals are normal. Not for people on insulin.

About $50–$100 per 2 weeksLearn more ↗

A headset used at home for depression, guided by an app. The first approved at-home brain stimulation treatment for depression.

Pulsetto, Truvaga and Nurosym send mild current through the neck or ear and are marketed for stress and sleep.

What to knowLow risk for most people, but don’t expect much more than relaxation. Not with a pacemaker or other implanted device. Slow breathing does a lot of the same for free.

What to knowFine if you find them helpful. They are relaxation tools, not treatments for anxiety or insomnia.

What “FDA-cleared” really means

  • It applies to one use. A watch can have an FDA-cleared ECG while its sleep and stress scores are not reviewed at all.
  • “FDA-registered” is not the same. Registration just means the company told the FDA it exists. It says nothing about whether the product works.
  • “Wellness” products aren’t reviewed. They can claim to help stress, sleep or energy, but not to treat a disease. Many popular gadgets, like most red light panels and vagus nerve stimulators, are in this group.
  • Bring the data to us. Readings are most useful when we look at them together, especially before you change a medicine.
