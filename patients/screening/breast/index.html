01

The most common cancer in women.

Breast cancer found while it is still only in the breast is one of the most treatable cancers there is. A mammogram can find it years before you could feel it.

99%of women are alive five years after a breast cancer found before it has spread beyond the breast.

02

Who should be screened, and when.

Your ageWhat I recommend
Under 40No routine mammograms at average risk. Start earlier if you are at higher risk (section 6).
40 to 74A mammogram every 2 years.
75 and olderThe studies did not include women this age, so the evidence is unclear. We decide together based on your health and what you want.

This applies to women, and to anyone assigned female at birth, at average risk. It includes women with dense breasts or with a mother or sister who had breast cancer, as long as there is no known gene change.

Why every 2 years and not every year? Some groups, including the American Cancer Society and radiology societies, recommend yearly mammograms. Yearly screening finds a few more cancers, and it brings about 50% more false alarms and overdiagnosis. I follow the US Preventive Services Task Force, which found the best balance at every 2 years. If you prefer yearly, tell us.

03

The test, and what dense breasts mean.

TestWhat it isWhat to know
Mammogram (2D)Low-dose X-ray pictures of each breast, pressed flat for a few seconds.The standard test. It takes about 15 minutes. The pressure is uncomfortable and brief.
3D mammogram (tomosynthesis)The same machine takes many thin pictures and builds a layered image.Works as well as 2D and leads to fewer callbacks.
Ultrasound or MRI added to a mammogramExtra imaging after a normal mammogram.For women at average risk, including those with dense breasts, there is not enough evidence to say whether this helps or harms. MRI is used for women at high risk.

Dense breasts

Breasts are a mix of fat and denser gland and fiber tissue. “Dense” means more of the second kind. It is normal, it has nothing to do with size or firmness, and you cannot feel it. About half of women have dense breasts.

Since September 2024, every mammogram report in the US must tell you whether your breasts are dense or not dense. It matters for two reasons: dense tissue and cancer both look white on a mammogram, so cancers are easier to miss, and dense tissue modestly raises the chance of getting breast cancer.

A mammogram is still the right first test. Whether to add another test depends on your overall risk, not on density alone. If your report says dense, ask us.

04

Benefits and risks.

The benefit is a lower chance of dying of breast cancer, and often simpler treatment because the cancer is found smaller.

The risks are real too.

RiskWhat it means
False alarmsOver a lifetime of mammograms every 2 years from 40 to 74, the average woman has more than one false alarm: a callback for something that turns out not to be cancer.
OverdiagnosisSome cancers found by screening would never have caused harm, and we cannot tell which ones. About 14 of every 1,000 women screened over a lifetime are treated for a cancer like that.
Missed cancersMammograms miss about 1 in 8 breast cancers, more in dense breasts. A normal result lowers your risk. It does not make it zero.
RadiationThe dose is very small.
05

What it means if you are called back.

About 1 in 10 women are called back after a screening mammogram. Fewer than 1 in 10 of those called back turn out to have cancer. Being called back is common, and it is most likely on your first mammogram, when there are no older pictures to compare.

StepWhat happens
More picturesA diagnostic mammogram takes closer views of one area. Often an ultrasound is done the same day. For most women, this settles it.
A short-term recheckSometimes the radiologist asks you to come back in 6 months to make sure an area that looks harmless is not changing.
A needle biopsyAbout 1 in 5 women who are called back need one. A small sample is taken with a needle, with numbing medicine, in the office. Many biopsies show no cancer.

Go to the follow-up. Women who get a false alarm are less likely to come back for their next mammogram. Please do not let one scare keep you away.

If cancer is found, we move quickly to get you to a breast surgeon and cancer team. Cancers found by screening are more often small and have not spread.

07

Screening is for people who feel fine.

If you notice a change in your breast, you need an evaluation now, at any age, even if your last mammogram was normal.

  • A new lump in the breast or armpit
  • Skin that dimples, puckers or thickens
  • A nipple that turns inward, or new discharge, especially if bloody
  • Redness, scaling or swelling of the breast or nipple that does not go away

Most breast lumps are not cancer. They still need to be checked. Men can get breast cancer too, and should report a lump.

08

Lower your risk.

The clearest link is alcohol: less is better. Staying active and avoiding weight gain after menopause help, and so does breastfeeding. More on the main page under foods and habits for breast health.
