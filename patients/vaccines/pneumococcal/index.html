Vaccine information

Pneumococcal vaccine

What I want my patients to know about the vaccine many people call “the pneumonia shot.”

Who
All adults 50 and older, and adults 19 to 49 with certain conditions
Doses
Usually one
Best time
Any time of year. It can be given with your flu shot.
Cost
$0 with Medicare Part B
01

Not quite “the pneumonia shot.”

This vaccine protects against one bacterium, Streptococcus pneumoniae, or pneumococcus. It is a common cause of pneumonia. When it gets into places that are normally germ-free, like the bloodstream, it causes what we call invasive disease, which is usually very serious and can be fatal.

Young children catch pneumococcus often, but older adults are the most likely to become seriously ill or die from it. The vaccine does not prevent pneumonia from viruses or other bacteria, so you still need your flu, COVID-19 and RSV vaccines.

02

Who should get it.

  • All adults 50 and older. Until late 2024 the starting age was 65, so many people in their 50s and early 60s have not heard yet.
  • Adults 19 to 49 who:
    • Smoke cigarettes or have alcohol use disorder
    • Have diabetes
    • Have chronic heart, lung (including asthma and COPD), liver or kidney disease
    • Have a weakened immune system, HIV, no spleen, or sickle cell disease
    • Have a cochlear implant or a spinal fluid leak
03

The number is the number of types.

Pneumococcus comes in many types, each with a slightly different outer coat. The number in each vaccine’s name is how many of those coats it teaches your immune system to recognize.

For most adults 50 and older who have never had a pneumococcal vaccine, it is one shot and you are finished.

Already had a pneumococcal vaccine years ago? The rules depend on which one and when. Bring your vaccine record and we will sort it out together.

04

What to expect.

Most people have no serious problems. Common side effects are a sore, red or swollen arm, fever or chills, tiredness, headache and muscle aches. They usually go away within about 2 days.

Like every vaccine, it works well but cannot prevent every case.
