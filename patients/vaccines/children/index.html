Vaccine information

Childhood vaccines

The routine vaccines for children from birth to 18, when each dose is due, and a checklist to see what your child needs next.

01

The schedule at a glance

These are the routine vaccines for healthy children. Some children need extra vaccines or a different schedule because of a health condition, travel, or vaccines missed along the way. We will sort that out together.

AgeVaccines
BirthHepatitis B (dose 1)
1 to 2 monthsHepatitis B (dose 2)
2 monthsRotavirus, DTaP, Hib, pneumococcal, polio (dose 1 of each)
4 monthsRotavirus, DTaP, Hib, pneumococcal, polio (dose 2 of each)
6 monthsDTaP and pneumococcal (dose 3). Rotavirus and Hib dose 3 for some brands
6 to 18 monthsHepatitis B and polio (dose 3)
12 to 15 monthsHib (last dose), pneumococcal (dose 4), MMR and chickenpox (dose 1)
12 to 23 monthsHepatitis A, 2 doses 6 months apart
15 to 18 monthsDTaP (dose 4)
4 to 6 yearsDTaP (dose 5), polio (dose 4), MMR and chickenpox (dose 2)
9 to 12 yearsHPV, 2 doses 6 to 12 months apart
11 to 12 yearsTdap, meningococcal ACWY (dose 1)
16 yearsMeningococcal ACWY (dose 2)

Every year from 6 months: flu vaccine. Children under 9 getting it for the first time need two doses, four weeks apart.

02

What does my child need next?

Enter your child's date of birth, then mark how many doses of each vaccine they have had. Your child's vaccine record is in MyChart, or ask us for a copy. Your answers stay on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

03

Also ask us about

RSV protection for babiesBabies get an antibody shot for their first RSV season, unless the mother had the RSV vaccine in pregnancy. Most babies need one or the other, not both. More in my RSV guide.
COVID-19Ask us whether your child should get it, especially if they have a health condition.
Meningococcal BAn extra meningitis vaccine that teens 16 to 18 can get. Ask us whether it makes sense for your teen.
Behind on vaccines?It is never too late to catch up, and you never need to start a series over. Bring the record and we will make a catch-up plan.

Educational information only, not medical advice for your child. Your child's clinician can tell you exactly what they need based on their health and vaccine history.

Federal childhood vaccine guidance changed in January 2026, and a federal court put those changes on hold in March 2026. This page follows the schedule of the American Academy of Pediatrics and the CDC schedule the court restored, which agree on the vaccines here. Last reviewed November 2026.
