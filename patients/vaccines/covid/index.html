Vaccine information

COVID-19 vaccine

What I want my patients to know about this season’s COVID-19 vaccine.

Who
All adults 18 and older
Doses
One this season. Two if you are 65 or older, six months apart.
Best time
Fall, at least 8 weeks after your last COVID-19 shot
Cost
$0 with Medicare Part B
01

Same sheriff, new wanted poster.

On my flu page I describe a vaccine as a wanted poster: it shows your immune system what the outlaw looks like before it rides into town. COVID-19 keeps changing its face, so each fall the vaccine is updated to match the strains going around. Last year’s poster still helps, but this year’s is a better likeness.

Older adults carry most of the risk. From October 2023 through August 2024, 70% of COVID-19 hospitalizations in the U.S. were in adults 65 and older.

02

Who should get it, and how many.

WhoThis season
Adults 18 to 64One dose
Adults 65 and olderTwo doses, about six months apart (at least two months)
Weakened immune systemYou may need extra doses. Ask us.
Pregnant or breastfeedingOne dose

CDC, the American Academy of Family Physicians and the Massachusetts Department of Public Health agree on these recommendations as of fall 2026.

Under 65 and healthy? The vaccine is still recommended for you. The FDA labels list people under 65 who have a risk condition, so a pharmacy may ask about your health. You can simply tell them; no proof is required.

Just had COVID-19? You can wait up to three months before getting vaccinated.

04

What to expect.

A sore arm, tiredness, headache, muscle aches or a low fever for a day or so are common.

A rare inflammation of the heart, called myocarditis, has been seen mostly in young men after mRNA vaccines. Spacing doses at least 8 weeks apart lowers that risk. If you develop chest pain, shortness of breath or a racing heartbeat in the days after a shot, get checked.

The vaccine cannot give you COVID-19 and does not change your DNA.
