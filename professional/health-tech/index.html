Smartwatches and rings

3

All-day trackers. A few features are FDA-cleared; most are wellness estimates.

Some features FDA-cleared

Apple Watch

Apple

A smartwatch. A few features are FDA-cleared medical functions: the ECG app and irregular rhythm alerts for atrial fibrillation, sleep apnea notifications, and since September 2025 hypertension notifications, which look at 30 days of optical sensor data. Everything else (sleep stages, VO2 max, HRV) is wellness.

EvidenceModerate

What I tell patientsThe AFib and sleep apnea features are worth acting on: get an ECG or a sleep study. A hypertension notification means check with a validated cuff; no notification does not rule hypertension out.

$250–$800Learn more ↗
General wellness

Oura and other smart rings

Oura, Samsung, Ultrahuman

Rings track sleep timing, resting heart rate, HRV, skin temperature and activity. Sleep staging is an estimate and differs from a sleep study.

EvidenceLimited

What I tell patientsGood for trends and habits, especially sleep timing and how alcohol or late meals affect resting heart rate. Not diagnostic. If a patient is worried by a "readiness" score, the trend matters more than any one night.

$300–$500, plus subscriptionLearn more ↗
ECG FDA-cleared; rest wellness

Whoop

Whoop

A screenless band built around strain, recovery and sleep. Its ECG feature was FDA-cleared in April 2025. Its Blood Pressure Insights drew an FDA warning letter in July 2025; the FDA closed the matter in June 2026 after Whoop changed the feature, so it remains a wellness estimate, not a cleared measurement.

EvidenceLimited

What I tell patientsFine as a fitness and sleep coach. Tell patients not to use its blood pressure numbers for decisions; use a validated cuff.

Subscription, about $200–$360 a yearLearn more ↗

Personal ECG

1

Record a rhythm strip when symptoms happen.

FDA-cleared

KardiaMobile personal ECG

AliveCor

A credit-card or pocket-size ECG: rest fingers on the pads for 30 seconds and the app records a single-lead (KardiaMobile, Card) or six-lead (6L) tracing. FDA-cleared AI flags atrial fibrillation, bradycardia, tachycardia and, on newer versions, other common rhythms. The tracing can be emailed or shared with us.

EvidenceStrong

What I tell patientsFor palpitations that come and go, a recording during the symptom beats a Holter that misses it. Great for AFib follow-up and checking rhythm after medication changes. A normal result with ongoing symptoms still needs a visit.

About $80–$150, optional subscriptionLearn more ↗

Blood pressure monitors

2

Home readings are the best way to diagnose and track high blood pressure.

FDA-cleared

Validated home BP cuff

Many brands; check validatebp.org

An upper-arm automatic cuff. Home monitoring is the best-studied way to diagnose and control hypertension.

EvidenceStrong

What I tell patientsStill the standard. Point patients to a device listed on validatebp.org, the right cuff size, and a week of twice-daily readings before a visit.

$40–$100Learn more ↗
FDA-cleared, over the counter

Hilo (Aktiia) cuffless BP band

Aktiia

A wrist band that estimates blood pressure from optical pulse-wave analysis while you are at rest. Cleared in July 2025 as the first over-the-counter cuffless BP monitor; it needs regular calibration with a cuff (included). US launch planned for 2026.

EvidenceLimited

What I tell patientsPromising for frequent readings and nighttime patterns. Until more independent data exist, confirm any decision-changing number with a validated cuff.

About $250–$300Learn more ↗

Glucose monitors

1

Sensors worn on the arm that read glucose every few minutes.

FDA-cleared, over the counter

Over-the-counter CGMs

Dexcom Stelo, Abbott Lingo and Libre Rio

Stelo (March 2024) was the first CGM cleared for sale without a prescription, followed by Abbott’s Lingo and Libre Rio. For adults 18+ not using insulin and without problematic hypoglycemia; no low-glucose alarms.

EvidenceModerate

What I tell patientsA great teaching tool for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes not on insulin: a two-week experiment shows what meals, walks and sleep do. For people without diabetes there are no outcome trials, and normal post-meal rises can cause needless worry.

About $50–$100 per 2-week sensorLearn more ↗

Hearing

FDA-authorized OTC hearing aid

AirPods Pro hearing aid feature

Apple

Software that turns AirPods Pro into an over-the-counter hearing aid for mild to moderate hearing loss, after an in-app hearing test.

EvidenceModerate

Nerve and brain stimulation

5

Devices that stimulate nerves or the brain with mild electrical current. The range runs from prescription treatments with good trials to wellness gadgets.

FDA-cleared, prescription

gammaCore

electroCore

A handheld non-invasive vagus nerve stimulator held on the neck. It has 6 FDA-cleared headache indications, including acute and preventive treatment of migraine (age 12+) and of cluster headache.

EvidenceModerate

What I tell patientsA real option for migraine or cluster headache when drugs fail or aren’t tolerated. Prescription and expensive; check coverage.

Prescription; high cash costLearn more ↗
General wellness

Consumer vagus nerve stimulators

Pulsetto, Truvaga, Nurosym

Ear-clip or neck devices that deliver mild electrical stimulation, marketed for stress, sleep and “vagal tone.” None is FDA-cleared for a disease. Most published evidence is for other devices or small trials of ear stimulation, not these products.

EvidenceVery limited

What I tell patientsLow risk for most people, but expect little beyond relaxation. Avoid with pacemakers, defibrillators or other implanted devices. Slow breathing does much of the same for free.

$250–$800Learn more ↗
General wellness

Vibration “nervous system” wearables

Apollo Neuro, Sensate

Wrist, ankle or chest devices that vibrate to promote calm. They do not stimulate the vagus nerve electrically, despite the marketing.

EvidenceVery limited

What I tell patientsHarmless. If a patient finds it calming, fine; it is not a treatment for anxiety or insomnia.

$300–$400Learn more ↗
FDA-cleared

Migraine neuromodulation devices

Cefaly, Nerivio, Relivion, SAVI Dual, gammaCore

Cefaly (forehead trigeminal stimulation) is over the counter for acute and preventive migraine in adults. Nerivio (upper-arm remote electrical neuromodulation) is prescription and cleared down to age 8. Others use occipital/trigeminal or magnetic stimulation.

EvidenceModerate

What I tell patientsWorth offering to patients with frequent migraine, medication overuse, pregnancy, or side effects from drugs. Response varies; a one-month trial is reasonable.

About $100–$500, varies by deviceLearn more ↗
FDA-approved (PMA), prescription

Flow FL-100 (home tDCS)

Flow Neuroscience

A headset that delivers transcranial direct current stimulation to the prefrontal cortex at home, guided by an app. Approved in December 2025 for adults with moderate to severe major depression who are not treatment-refractory, alone or with medication. US launch 2026.

EvidenceModerate

What I tell patientsA new non-drug option for patients who don’t want, or don’t tolerate, antidepressants. Prescription, with remote supervision; coverage unclear.

Light therapy

1

Red and near-infrared light.

Some devices FDA-cleared

Red light therapy (photobiomodulation)

Masks, panels, caps and wands; many brands

Red (~630–660 nm) and near-infrared (~830–850 nm) light. Specific devices are FDA-cleared for specific uses, such as hair growth in androgenetic alopecia or wrinkles, not for “red light” in general. Evidence is moderate for hair loss and some skin aging; weak for energy, thyroid, testosterone, fat loss or longevity.

EvidenceLimited

What I tell patientsLow risk with an FDA-cleared device used as directed. Protect the eyes. Steer patients away from expensive full-body panels sold for wellness claims.

$100–$500 for masks; panels often moreLearn more ↗

Sleep

2

Devices that change the sleep environment or the brain’s path into sleep.

General wellness; FDA filing in progress

Eight Sleep Pod

Eight Sleep

A water-cooled and heated mattress cover with separate sides, sensors for heart rate, breathing and sleep stages, and an app that adjusts temperature through the night. Sold as a wellness product; the company began pursuing FDA clearance for sleep apnea detection and mitigation in 2025.

EvidenceLimited

What I tell patientsGreat for hot sleepers, night sweats around menopause, and couples who run at different temperatures. A cooler bed is a real sleep lever. It is expensive and is not a treatment for sleep apnea: loud snoring or gasping still needs a sleep study.

About $2,600–$5,000+, plus about $200 a yearLearn more ↗
General wellness

Elemind

Elemind Technologies

A headband that reads EEG and plays bone-conducted sound timed to the alpha rhythm to help you fall asleep, from MIT-trained neuroscientists. Sold as a wellness device, not FDA-cleared. In the company’s trial, 76% fell asleep faster, by about 48% on average.

EvidenceLimited

What I tell patientsInteresting and low risk, but the studies are small and company-run. For insomnia, CBT-I comes first; this could be an add-on for sleep-onset trouble.

$349, plus optional membershipLearn more ↗

Apps

6

Prescription apps with FDA clearance. For everyday wellness apps I recommend, see my patient apps page.

FDA-cleared, prescription

SleepioRx and DaylightRx

Big Health

Two prescription apps that deliver CBT on a phone: SleepioRx (CBT-I) for insomnia disorder and DaylightRx, the first FDA-cleared digital treatment for generalized anxiety disorder. Both cleared in 2024. There is now a Medicare payment pathway for FDA-cleared digital mental health treatments.

EvidenceModerate

What I tell patientsCBT-I is first-line for chronic insomnia and most patients can’t get a therapist for it. This is a practical way to prescribe it. DaylightRx is an option for anxiety while waiting for therapy, or instead of a medicine.

Prescription; coverage variesLearn more ↗
FDA-cleared, prescription

Rejoyn

Otsuka and Click Therapeutics

The first FDA-cleared prescription app for adjunctive treatment of major depressive disorder symptoms, used alongside an antidepressant. Built on cognitive-emotional training exercises.

EvidenceLimited

What I tell patientsAn add-on, not a replacement for medicine or therapy. Reasonable for motivated patients with partial response.

Prescription; coverage variesLearn more ↗
FDA-cleared, prescription

NightWare

NightWare

Software on an Apple Watch that senses the heart rate and movement changes of a nightmare and gently vibrates to interrupt it without waking the person. Cleared for sleep disturbance from nightmare disorder or PTSD.

EvidenceLimited

What I tell patientsAn option for veterans and others with PTSD nightmares, alongside prazosin or therapy. Needs an Apple Watch and iPhone.

Prescription; VA and some insurers coverLearn more ↗
FDA-authorized, prescription

EndeavorRx

Akili

A prescription video game for children 8 to 17 with ADHD, designed to improve attention.

What I tell patientsA supplement to behavioral therapy and medicine, not a substitute. Kids tend to like it; effects on daily function are modest.

MamaLift Plus

Marigold Health

The first FDA-cleared prescription app for mild to moderate postpartum depression, delivering CBT-based lessons.

EvidenceLimited

What I tell patientsHelpful for new parents who can’t get to therapy. Moderate to severe symptoms or any safety concern still need direct care.

FDA-cleared birth control app

Natural Cycles

Natural Cycles

The first FDA-cleared birth control app. It uses temperature, from a thermometer, Oura Ring, Apple Watch or its own wristband, to predict fertile days. Since 2025 it can be sold over the counter or prescribed.

EvidenceModerate

What I tell patientsA hormone-free option for motivated patients who will use condoms or abstain on red days. Less effective than an IUD or implant; not a good fit if a pregnancy would be a serious problem.

For the office

1

Tools for clinicians, not for patients to buy.

FDA-cleared, for clinicians

Eko digital stethoscopes

Eko Health: CORE 500, CORE 300, CORE attachment

A digital stethoscope with up to 40x amplification and noise cancellation that records heart sounds to an app. The CORE 500 adds a 3-lead ECG. Eko’s FDA-cleared AI flags structural murmurs, AFib and, since 2024, low ejection fraction in about 15 seconds. Cleared for healthcare professionals only, not for patients to buy for home use.

EvidenceModerate

What I tell patientsFor our clinic, not patients. Useful for murmurs in noisy rooms, teaching, hearing loss, and screening for low EF at a routine visit. A flag means order an echo; it doesn’t replace one. The AI features need an Eko+ subscription; health systems license the Sensora platform, which has a Category III CPT code.

CORE 500 about $380–$490; attachment about $225–$260; Eko+ yearlyLearn more ↗

Five questions for any new device

  1. What exactly is FDA-cleared? Often one feature or one use, not the whole product or its ads.
  2. Who ran the studies? Small, company-run trials without a sham arm are a starting point, not proof.
  3. What would change? If no result would change what we do, the data may just add worry.
  4. What does it cost over a year? Subscriptions, sensors and gel pads add up.
  5. Is there a free version? Slow breathing, a sleep schedule, a walk after meals and a $50 cuff do a lot.
